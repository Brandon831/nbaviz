Data: ESPN play-by-play via sportsdataverse, 2022-23 onward. Shot locations are ESPN's own, so zone totals will not match NBA.com exactly. Free throws, heaves beyond 40 feet from the hoop, and All-Star games are left out. Regular season includes play-in games. The NBA Cup final is left out of regular-season totals, as in the league’s official stats, and shown as a badge instead. Games played counts games a player appears in the play-by-play.

Zone color shrinks small samples toward the league average, so a zone with a handful of shots will not light up on luck alone. The numbers in each zone and in the table are raw. Points per shot counts a made three as 3 and a made two as 2, so a corner three that goes in 38% of the time and a mid-range shot that goes in 57% of the time are worth the same. Shot selection is the points per shot a league-average shooter would get from the same mix of zones; shot making is how far the player is above or below that on those shots.

Shot type, shot creation, home or away and last-games filters use ESPN’s own play descriptions and dates. The league baseline follows the shot type, shot creation and location filters but not the last-games filter. Shot creation stands in for tracking data, which ESPN does not publish: catch-and-shoot style means a plain or running jump shot, and off the dribble means a pull-up, step-back, fadeaway or turnaround jumper or any shot ESPN calls a drive (driving layups, dunks, floaters and the like). It works best on threes: in 2025-26, 98% of made threes labeled as jump shots were assisted, against 44% of made pull-up and step-back threes. Mid-range is less clear, because ESPN labels about nine in ten mid-range jumpers as pull-ups or fadeaways. Assists are only recorded on made shots, so “Assisted” is a share of made field goals, not a filter. Shot-profile twins compare the share of each player’s attempts that come from each of the 14 zones.

How the badges are decided

League awards (gold). End-of-season awards from the league’s official voting results: MVP, Defensive Player of the Year, Rookie of the Year, Sixth Man of the Year, Most Improved Player, Clutch Player of the Year and Finals MVP. Each season has its own set, and the season in progress has none yet.

All-league teams. All-NBA, All-Defensive and All-Rookie First, Second and Third Team selections from the same releases. The icon color shows the tier: gold for the First Team, silver for the Second, bronze for the Third.

Top-10 statistical leaders. One badge for every category on the League Leaders tab in which a player finished in the top 10 of a season: points, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks, minutes, field goal %, 3-point %, free throw %, points per shot, points per 36 minutes, 3-pointers made, offensive rebounds and turnovers (most). The number is the player’s league rank, and #1 is gold, #2 and #3 are silver. For finished seasons this is the final regular-season ranking; for the current season it is the ranking right now and can change after every game. Per-game lists include players who appeared in at least 70% of their team’s games. Percentage lists and points per shot need 300 made field goals, 82 made threes or 125 made free throws over a full season, scaled to games played during the current season. Points per 36 minutes needs 70% of team games and at least 15 minutes per game. Players with the same value share a rank, and everyone ranked in the top 10 gets a badge, which can mean more than ten players in a category when there is a tie for 10th. Rankings come from ESPN box scores and follow the same rules as the League Leaders tab, so the current-season badges always match that tab.

Division and conference. Best regular-season winning percentage among the teams in the group: final standings for finished seasons, current standings for the season in progress. Teams with exactly the same winning percentage tie, and every tied team gets the badge (marked T-1st). The NBA’s own tiebreakers are not applied, and early in a season teams that have played different numbers of games can tie. The team badges use the team shown next to the player’s name, which for a traded player is the team they took the most shots for.

NBA Cup final. The player appeared in that season’s Cup championship game.

Badges describe the whole regular season and do not change with the Games filter. Award sources: NBA Communications voting-results releases and NBA.com.
